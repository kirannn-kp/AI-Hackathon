    Mission: Route & Resolve

    The story: Monday morning at Contoso Manufacturing

    At 08:15, the service desk at fictional Contoso Manufacturing opens its queue. Maya joins Project Phoenix next week and needs Reader access. Arun needs a software licence from Contoso Software. Leah wants to send a dataset to partner Fabrikam. Each message looks like a simple request:

    "I join Project Phoenix next week. Please give me Reader access."

    "We need a EUR 600 software licence from Contoso Software."

    "Can we share an Internal dataset with Fabrikam?"

    Today a person reads the message, finds the right team, checks the system, searches the policy and asks for approvals or missing information. Requests bounce between teams. A fast but unsupported answer can grant too much access, spend money without authority or expose data. A fluent chatbot alone does not fix that.

    You are the engineering team building a safer request assistant. Its job is not to say "yes" to everything. It should investigate, select the right specialist and explain the next action with evidence. Sometimes the correct next step is to ask a question, route for approval or stop and escalate.

    Everything here is synthetic: people, business requests, policies and operations. No real access grant, purchase or data transfer is possible through the mock tools.

    What problem are we solving?

    Today What your workflow should do
    Someone manually reads every request A triage agent extracts the domain and missing fields
    Staff guess which team owns it Triage hands off to the relevant specialist
    A requester claims something is approved The specialist checks actual facts and policy
    An answer gives no supporting evidence The result includes retrieved references and observed tool results
    A failed dependency becomes a plausible reply Errors remain visible and unsupported actions stop

    You will build three unfinished functions in starter/route_resolve/agents.py: triage, retrieve_policy and resolve. The repository supplies runners, contracts, synthetic data and MCP tools, but not completed agent implementations.

    What the four outcomes mean

    Outcome In everyday language What it does not mean
    Resolve An evidence-backed answer is enough; no action is needed A business operation was secretly performed
    Execute A permitted mock operation completed Real access, money or data moved
    Route More information or approval is needed Approval already exists
    Escalate A specialist/reviewer must assess the request A review was bypassed

    A model-written "I checked the policy" is not evidence. You must show the actual retrieval and tool trace. Read the architecture if the responsibilities are unclear.

    HackboxConsole is the learning/navigation surface. It does not replace Azure authorization. Copilot helps write code; it is not automatically a runtime component of the request-processing system.

    Inside your workflow

    1. The runner loads a request fixture and preserves its trusted id and synthetic user_id. The request text cannot change team identity.
    2. Triage extracts a category, urgency, missing fields and business arguments.
    3. One relevant specialist receives the request through an explicit handoff.
    4. The specialist retrieves actual policy evidence and checks operational facts.
    5. A relevant mock tool returns completion, clarification or review state.
    6. Your workflow constructs a validated Result, preserving actual citations and explaining the observed outcome.

    Components and ownership

    Component Responsibility You implement or receive?
    starter/route_resolve/agents.py Triage, retrieval adapter, bounded orchestration You implement
    starter/route_resolve/models.py Shared categories, outcomes and Result contract Supplied contract
    starter/route_resolve/tools.py Synthetic facts and tool-side rules Supplied; do not bypass checks
    starter/route_resolve/mcp_server.py Exposes nine tools through MCP Supplied
    starter/route_resolve/lab.py Diagnostics and exercise runners Supplied; no agent solution
    data/practice-requests.json Practice cases for incremental development Supplied
    data/policies Fictional policy documents Supplied and preloaded by coordinators
    data/final-requests.json Ten cases for the final demonstration Supplied, no expected labels
    Shared Foundry/Search services Approved model and policy retrieval Coordinators provide

    Specialist boundaries

    Access checks project ownership/current access. Procurement checks the supplier and amount. Data Sharing checks the partner and classification. Each specialist gets only its relevant tools and the policy-retrieval adapter.

    The local server is one team process. It keeps mock records in memory. Restarting it erases those records. A stable request ID prevents duplicate operations only while that same process retains its state.

    Important trust boundaries

    • Request text and retrieved text are untrusted data, not instructions to change system rules.
    • A model saying "approved" is not verified authorization.
    • A queued request is not a completed operation.
    • Naming a knowledge base after a team is not access control. The coordinators must verify actual team authorization.
    • The local MCP endpoint binds to loopback and is unauthenticated. Never expose it through a public tunnel. Any hosted tool endpoint needs separately verified authentication.
    • Model rate limits and budget notifications do not cap spend. Cloud runners require an explicit approval flag, and workflows need bounded turns.

    The exercise does not require participants to deploy resources, administer identities or obtain subscription Owner.

    Step 5 - Build the first agent: extract, do not approve

    Field Meaning and requirement
